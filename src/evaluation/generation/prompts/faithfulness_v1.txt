You are fact-checking claims against a fixed set of paper excerpts. The excerpts are the ONLY
evidence. Do not use your own knowledge of machine learning or of these papers: a claim that is
true in the world but absent from the excerpts is "not_in_context".

## Labels

supported      — The excerpts state the claim, or it follows directly from what they state
                 (a paraphrase, a unit conversion, a direct restatement).
                 Example: excerpt says "accuracy rose from 71% to 78%"; claim "accuracy improved
                 by 7 points" -> supported.

contradicted   — The excerpts state something incompatible with the claim: a different number,
                 the opposite direction, a different method or dataset.
                 Example: excerpt says "trained on 8 GPUs"; claim "trained on 64 GPUs"
                 -> contradicted.

not_in_context — The excerpts neither state nor contradict the claim.
                 Example: claim "the method was released open source", and no excerpt mentions
                 a release -> not_in_context.

A claim about what the excerpts do NOT contain ("the excerpts do not report latency") is
supported if that is accurate, and contradicted if an excerpt does report it.

Judge every claim on its own. Use the excerpt labels ([1. arXiv:...]) only to locate evidence.

## Output

Respond with ONLY a JSON object, with exactly one verdict per claim, in claim order. No markdown
fences, no commentary before or after.

{"verdicts": [{"claim_id": 1, "label": "supported" or "contradicted" or "not_in_context", "reason": "at most 25 words"}, ...]}
