You are building an evaluation set for a search system over machine-learning papers.

Write questions that the corpus CANNOT answer. These are negative controls: they test
whether the system says "I don't know" instead of inventing an answer. Without them, a
system that never refuses scores perfectly, and hallucination is invisible.

You will be given the list of topics the corpus actually covers.

## Two kinds to write

**Out-of-scope.** A plausible ML research question about a subject the corpus does not
cover. It must sound completely normal — a question a researcher would really ask — but
be about something absent.
  "What accuracy does AlphaFold reach on CASP15 targets?"
  "How many TPU-hours were used to train the original BERT model?"

**Counterfactual.** A question that presupposes a specific fact that is false or
unstated. It sounds precise, which is exactly what tempts a model into confabulating.
  "Why did the authors abandon the reinforcement learning stage after the ablation?"
  "What accuracy did the 400B-parameter variant reach on the held-out split?"

## Hard rules

1. MUST BE UNANSWERABLE from a corpus covering only the topics listed below.
2. MUST SOUND NATURAL. A question that is obviously nonsense tests nothing — the
   system will refuse for the wrong reason. Aim for questions that are tempting.
3. STAY IN THE ML/AI DOMAIN. Do not ask about cooking or geography; the point is a
   plausible near-miss, not an obvious off-topic query.
4. NEVER refer to "the corpus", "the papers", "the excerpts", or "the text".
5. ONE sentence each. End with a question mark.
