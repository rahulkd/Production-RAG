You are building an evaluation set for a search system over machine-learning papers.

You will be shown ONE excerpt from the body of a paper. Write ONE question that a
working ML researcher would genuinely type into a paper search tool, and that this
excerpt answers.

## What a real researcher question looks like

Researchers search because they have a problem. They ask about mechanisms, numbers,
tradeoffs, and design choices — in their own words, using the field's standard
terminology, because they have not read this paper yet.

GOOD (write questions like these):
  "How much memory does LoRA save compared to full fine-tuning at 7B scale?"
  "What stops the reward model from being gamed during preference training?"
  "Which datasets were used to evaluate the retrieval component?"
  "Why does the method need a separate target network?"
  "What happens to accuracy when the number of experts is increased?"

BAD (never write questions like these):
  "According to the passage, what are the three components?"   <- refers to the text
  "What does this paper propose?"                              <- vague, matches anything
  "What is TriAdaptLoRA-Gamma-v2?"                             <- quotes a coined name
  "In Section 3.2, what is the value of lambda?"               <- refers to structure
  "What did the authors observe in their experiments?"         <- meta, not substantive

## Hard rules

1. ANSWERABLE FROM THIS EXCERPT ALONE. Do not ask about anything not stated here.
2. NEVER refer to "the excerpt", "the passage", "this paper", "the text", "the study",
   "the authors", "the figure", or any section number. The person asking has not seen
   this excerpt — they are trying to FIND it.
3. USE DIFFERENT WORDS. Do not reuse distinctive multi-word phrases from the excerpt.
   Prefer the field's generic terminology over names the authors coined. If the excerpt
   says "our Adaptive Freezing Module", ask about "freezing layers during training".
4. BE SPECIFIC ENOUGH TO HAVE ONE ANSWER. "What is discussed?" is useless; "which
   optimizer was used for the ablation?" is useful.
5. ONE question. One sentence. End with a question mark.
6. Do NOT ask about the paper's title, its abstract, its authors, or its affiliations.
